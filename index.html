Design Thinking — Why MODE?

📋 Short Description (Ready for Contra Submission Form)

MODE — A Video.js v10 Skin for Every Occasion: Video.js powers video across millions of websites, yet a single static skin rarely fits every viewing context. Built on Video.js v10 custom elements (@videojs/html v10.3.0), MODE introduces five context-aware presets that adapt typography, geometry, color science, and interactive tooling to how people actually watch: Movie (deep crimson velvet and champagne gold with film grain and room dimming), Music (frosted cyberpunk glass that samples live video edge pixels at 15fps to reflect ambient colors onto the player chrome alongside a Web Audio spectrum visualizer and orbiting perimeter spark), Study (uncluttered pastel alabaster and slate with native Video.js v10 <media-i18n> localization across 11 languages, live dual-caption subtitle translation, a searchable transcript & timestamp bookmark drawer with a YouTube Chat-style Pop-out window, and A–B loop repeat), Chill (floating pill-island liquid glass with sunset bokeh and a Zen sleep fade timer), and Skeleton (a 75% transparent black wireframe with 24fps SMPTE frame timecode). Controls stay hidden until hovered—unless pinned via the built-in "Always Visible Controls" setting—and all mode animations can be toggled on/off or automatically calmed via prefers-reduced-motion.

1. Context-First Craft

Every skin makes a trade-off between personality and restraint. Rather than forcing one compromise, MODE treats the Video.js v10 media store and custom elements as a living state machine. Switching from data-mode="movie" to data-mode="study" doesn't just swap colors—it dynamically updates the Video.js v10 <media-icon family="..."> set, adjusts concentric border radii (outer = inner + padding), and reveals occasion-specific tools like A–B looping or live audio-reactive canvases.

2. Real-Time Edge Color & Sonic Synthesis

In Music Mode, an offscreen 32×18 canvas samples the top, bottom, left, and right edge pixels of the active <video> element at 15fps, boosting chroma and projecting --mode-edge-* CSS custom properties onto the player borders and ambient halo. Paired with a Web Audio API AnalyserNode and Arcane Domain's signature perimeter spark comet trail, the control bar becomes an extension of the music video itself.

🎵 Original Music Credits: Track 2 video and song are "The Storm" from the electronic album INDIVISION (Spotify) by Arcane Domain. The High-Key Solar stress test (Scenario 4) features "Time in Space" (Spotify) by Arcane Domain (starting ~1 minute into the track).

3. Polyglot Learning with Video.js v10 SDK

Study Mode showcases Video.js v10's built-in <media-i18n> element and official locale bundles (@videojs/html/i18n/locales/all), allowing instant live switching of UI labels and RTL layout (including Arabic dir="rtl") without reloading. Students can also enable Dual-Caption Translation, search and click timestamped cues or pin notes in the transcript drawer, pop out the transcript into a standalone YouTube Chat-style window (↗ Popout), and lock A–B Loop segments on the timeline.

25 + 2 POINT RUBRIC VERIFICATION

Accessibility, Usability & Polish Audit

✓
Readable Over Bright & Dark Video

Tested against both dark gothic footage and our High-Key Solar stress test. Even the 75% transparent Skeleton mode pairs a 1px solid rgba(0,0,0,0.95) outer wireframe with an inner white hairline and text shadow so controls never vanish over pure white or pitch black frames.

✓
Full Keyboard Access & Visible Focus

Every button, slider, mode pill, and nested <media-menu> is reachable via Tab / Arrow keys, closes cleanly on Escape, preserves a high-contrast 2px solid var(--_accent-color) :focus-visible outline, and supports hotkeys (Space, K, M, F, C, J/L, 0-9).

✓
Responsive @container Layout & 44px Touch Targets

Uses CSS container queries (@container mode-player) so the player adapts to its own width (from 320px mobile embeds up to 4K fullscreen), automatically expanding interactive targets to 44×44px on coarse pointers (@media (pointer: coarse)).

✓
Calm on Request (prefers-reduced-motion + Manual FX Toggle)

Every mode includes an explicit FX: ON / OFF switch in the top bar and settings menu, and automatically disables all canvas animations, film grain, and transitions when the OS reports @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce).
